# Second erratum — the review was right about provenance, and the data now says so

The first erratum corrected four numbers and, as the reviewer observed, every one of them was
arithmetic. This one corrects a category, which is the kind the same party cannot audit in itself.
It is written after the second independent review and it concedes its leading finding in full.

---

## What was wrong

Fifteen evidence rows across eleven entries carried `by: round1-arm-B`, `by: round2-arm-C` and the
like, with timestamps rounded to the minute that arm had run — `2026-09-15T09:30:00.000Z`.

**No arm ever ran a writing verb, in any round.** Every one of those rows was typed on 2026-09-16 by
the author of this tool, reading the arm's report, and dated to when the arm ran rather than to when
the row was written.

Verified independently before accepting the finding: `grep` of `by: round` across `captured/`
against `git log --diff-filter=A` for each file. All fifteen files entered git on 14–16 September.

## What it cost

1. **Trust.** `experientialTrust` counts distinct `by` values as independent parties. Three entries
   stood at `confirmed` on one party's transcription — precisely the self-confirmation the
   independence rule had been added days earlier to stop. The rule could not bite, because the field
   it read was a free string.
2. **The arrival replay.** It counts an entry as pre-existing if its first `at:` predates the run
   being replayed. A row dated into the run it was transcribed from reads as help that was already
   there.
3. **Two published numbers, both of which are zero.**

| claim as published | honest figure |
|---|---|
| mining eleven entries moved arrivals by **two calls** | **0** — 244 before, 244 after; both of "the two" were one backdated entry |
| nine delivery addresses moved arrivals **295 → 316** | **0** — 244 → 244; the raw count is right and counts entries that did not exist during the runs |

The second is the one that matters: `arrivesAt` shipped yesterday and was reported to the reviewer
as the day's result. It has bought nothing measurable. The idea may still be right — a fact is
needed where you meet the symptom, not where it is about — but nothing has been shown to arrive by
delivery address that would not have arrived anyway.

## What changed in the tool

* **`--by` and `--at` are refused at the CLI.** The tool writes both: `by` from the session
  identity, which a writer cannot choose for itself. The refusal names what to do instead.
* **`--from <path>` is the honest shape** for a claim read out of a report. The path must exist, so
  a reader can open it and disagree — the property a typed author name never had.
* **`partiesOf` counts `from` ahead of `by`.** One author transcribing three arm reports is three
  parties, because those are three observations badly recorded. One author writing three rows
  unaided is one party. Rows carrying neither field — the 121 written before any of this existed —
  each count as their own party, so nothing in the corpus was re-graded by the rule landing.
* **The fifteen rows were relabelled, not deleted** (`scripts/relabel-transcriptions-2026-09-16.mjs`,
  a committed migration with a dry run). Each now names the report it was read out of and carries
  the commit time of the row rather than the arm's clock.
* Eight new tests. 232 → 240.

## Where we did not follow the recommendation, and why

The review says the corpus "can hold *arm B's report says X*; it must not hold *arm B confirmed X*",
and treats the three `confirmed` entries as resting on one party. We kept them at `confirmed`,
because after relabelling the party they rest on is the **artefact**, not the transcriber: three
distinct reports, each on disk, each openable.

We think that is consistent with the review's own reading — it cites `KB-27B4CD10` as rediscovered
by two armless arms and `KB-5ADBFB34` by three, and calls that the only demand signal in this
project uncontaminated by its author. The rediscoveries are real; what was wrong was the recording.
**If the reviewer disagrees, this is the item to say so on**, and the fix is one line in
`partiesOf`.

## What the reviewer said about the first erratum, which we accept

That it caught only arithmetic; that a rerun by anyone catches arithmetic; and that what it missed
was every question of whether a row *counts* — including the 246 it quoted as reassurance, which was
the very figure that zeroed the same afternoon's headline.

That is exactly what happened, and it is why this erratum exists and why the provenance change went
first rather than the redesign.
